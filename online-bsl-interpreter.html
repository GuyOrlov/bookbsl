Remote BSL interpreting

Booking an online BSL interpreter

Online interpreting can work well when the platform, camera and meeting access are prepared properly. Use this guide for Zoom, Microsoft Teams and other remote meetings.

Send the meeting link

Give the interpreter the correct platform, joining link, passcode if required, start time and a contact person in case there is a technical problem.

Make the interpreter easy to see

Participants should know how to pin or spotlight the interpreter. Avoid layouts where the interpreter becomes a very small video tile.

Check cameras and lighting

The Deaf participant and interpreter need a clear view of each other. Use stable cameras, good lighting and enough bandwidth for smooth video.

Allow for preparation

Share the agenda, names, slides and specialist vocabulary before the session just as you would for an in-person booking.
