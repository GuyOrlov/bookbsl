BookBSL data & evidence

The numbers behind better BSL booking.

The UK has a limited pool of registered communication professionals. These figures show the scale of demand, why specialist support can be difficult to secure, and why early booking matters.

Checked 11 September 2026. Population estimates come from different sources and measure different things, so they should not be treated as interchangeable.

Why early booking matters

A large community. A small specialist workforce.

Population figures and current NRCPD registration numbers show why BSL and specialist communication support can become difficult to secure at short notice.

87,000
Deaf BSL users in the UK

Estimated by the British Deaf Association and cited in current government BSL reporting.

1,664
Registered Sign Language Interpreters

Current NRCPD registration total. The register also includes dual registrations.

≈52:1
Deaf BSL users per registered SLI

A simple scale comparison, not an interpreter caseload or availability measure.

32
Registered Speech-to-Text Reporters

A much smaller specialist register, which helps explain why STTR availability can be tight.

BSL: aim for 2–3+ weeks

Published UK guidance recommends booking registered BSL interpreters at least two to three weeks ahead where possible.

STTR: secure early

STTRs are described as high demand; Stagetext recommends securing live captioning ideally at least three weeks ahead and before tickets go on sale.

Major events: start 3–6 months early

BookBSL planning advice: for fixed-date conferences, festivals or events needing scarce specialists or a larger team, start planning months ahead. This is not a national minimum rule.

The 52:1 figure divides the published estimate of 87,000 Deaf BSL users by 1,664 registered Sign Language Interpreters. It is included only to show population-to-workforce scale. It does not mean each interpreter serves 52 people, and it does not measure regional availability, working hours, specialism or demand.

Do not add these categories together to estimate unique professionals. NRCPD states that its totals include dual registrations, so one person can appear in more than one register.

UK Deaf & BSL population

How many people are Deaf or use BSL in the UK?

There is no single number that means “the Deaf population”. Hearing loss, cultural Deaf identity, BSL use and BSL as a main language are different measures.

12m
Deaf or hard of hearing in the UK

The UK Government’s 2026 BSL report cites the RNID estimate of around 12 million people.

UK Government source ↗
151k
BSL users in the UK

British Deaf Association estimate quoted in the latest UK Government BSL report. This includes deaf and hearing BSL users.

UK Government source ↗
87k
Deaf BSL users in the UK

The same government report quotes the BDA estimate that about 87,000 of the 151,000 BSL users are deaf.

UK Government source ↗
22k
BSL as a main language

Census 2021 recorded 22,000 usual residents aged 3+ in England and Wales with BSL as their main language.

ONS source ↗

Important: the 22,000 census figure is not the total number of BSL users. It only counts people who reported BSL as their main language in England and Wales.

Rest of the world

Global hearing-loss context from WHO.

WHO data describes hearing loss globally. It does not provide the UK BSL-user estimate used above, so BookBSL keeps the global and UK figures separate.

430m+

People need hearing rehabilitation

WHO says more than 5% of the world’s population — around 430 million people — require rehabilitation for disabling hearing loss.

WHO source ↗
2.5bn

Projected to have some hearing loss by 2050

WHO projects that nearly 2.5 billion people will have some degree of hearing loss by 2050, with more than 700 million requiring hearing rehabilitation.
